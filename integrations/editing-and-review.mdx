## Overview

Every article Pierview writes opens in the content editor, a full-screen workspace. The bar at the top holds the article's title and save status on the left, and **Export**, **Copy** and **Publish** on the right. Formatting (text style, bold, italic, lists, links) sits in the toolbar just below it, and every button shows what it does and its keyboard shortcut when you hover over it. The main menu is folded away to give the article room: hover over it to see each section's name, or click **Content** to go back. From there you can change any passage with AI, use the **Edit with chat** panel on the left for quick edits in plain words ("shorten the introduction", "make the tone friendlier"), and leave comments and suggestions for your team. Edits you accept teach Pierview your preferences, so later articles need fewer changes.

## Edit a passage with AI

1. Select the text you want to change.
2. Click **Edit with AI** in the menu that appears above the selection.
3. Describe the change, or pick a shortcut: **Shorter**, **Simpler**, **More specific**, **Match brand voice** or **Fix grammar**.
4. Review the proposed text. The original is struck through and the new text is shown beside it.
5. Click **Accept** to apply it, **Try again** for another version, **Reject** to discard it, or **Save as suggestion** to leave it for a teammate to decide.

Links, formatting and the article's spelling conventions are kept. If you change the selected text before accepting, select it again to get a fresh edit.

## Pierview learns from your edits

When an accepted edit reflects a lasting preference (for example "avoid the word leverage" or "use sentence case in headings"), Pierview saves it as a writing rule in your Brand Kit. A notification shows the rule with **Undo** and **Edit**. One-off changes, such as correcting a fact, are not saved.

Learned rules appear under **Brand Kit > Foundations > Learned from your edits**. Each rule links to the article it came from, and you can edit or remove it there. Every article Pierview writes afterwards follows your active rules, alongside the writing rules you entered yourself.

<Info>
  Pierview keeps up to 40 active learned rules per organization. When a new rule refines an existing one, it replaces it instead of adding a duplicate.
</Info>

## Chat with the editor

The chat sits to the left of the article. Drag the divider to give it more or less room, or click **Hide chat** to give the article the full width. It takes requests in plain language, such as "make the introduction more direct" or "write a meta description under 155 characters", and reads the article as it is on screen, including unsaved changes. The chat can also search the web and read pages, for example to check a figure, update an out-of-date fact or find a source to cite. Pages it used are listed under its reply. While it works, the chat shows what it is thinking about and which changes it is drafting.

The switch under the message box sets how changes are applied:

- **Manual** (the default): every change waits for you, shown in the article where it would go: the current text struck through and the new text in green. Hover over a change and choose **Accept suggestion** or **Decline suggestion**, answer it from the chat card, or click **Apply all** to accept every change at once.
- **Auto**: each change is applied as soon as the chat proposes it. Every applied change has an **Undo** button.

Pierview checks every proposed change before you see it: an edit that would remove a section, drop a link or match the wrong passage is not offered. Click **New conversation** to start over.

## Content score

The **Score** tab on the right rates the article from 0 to 100 as you edit. It checks length, structure, paragraph length, sources, links to your own site, an FAQ section, the meta title and description lengths, the URL slug and structured data. Each check that does not pass says what to change. Checks that do not apply, such as meta fields on a LinkedIn post, are left out of the score.

### Target keyword

Each article targets one Google keyword: the phrase you want it to rank for. You can name it when you create the article. If you don't, Pierview picks one from the title and outline, choosing the phrase people search for most, and writes the article, meta title, meta description and URL slug around it.

The **Keyword** checks show where the keyword appears: the title, the opening paragraph, at least one subheading, the meta title, the meta description and the URL slug. A close variant counts. There is no keyword density target, since repeating a keyword to hit a number makes an article read worse without helping it rank.

Edit the meta title, meta description, slug, author and structured data in the **SEO** tab. Character counts show the recommended range.

## Publish

Click **Publish** in the top-right corner of the article to choose a connected CMS and publish, or save a draft. The destination you used last is selected for you.

## Comments and suggestions

Select text and click **Comment** to start a thread, or **Suggest** to propose replacement text. Highlights in the article show where each comment and suggestion sits, and the **Comments** tab lists them in the order they appear.

- Reply to a thread, resolve it when it is done, or turn on **Show resolved** to see past threads.
- Accept or decline a suggestion by hovering over it in the article, or from its card. Accepting replaces the text in the article.
- If the commented text is later rewritten, the thread stays in the list with a note that the text is no longer in the article.
